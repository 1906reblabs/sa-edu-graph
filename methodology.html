Verification & trust levels

Every listing carries two independent signals: a trust level (where the resource comes from) and a quality score (how complete and usable it is). Neither is a judgement on the resource's academic content — both describe how confident we are in the link and its provenance.

Trust level

LevelMeaning
AOfficial primary source — the resource is hosted directly by the government department, assessment body, or exam board that produced it.
BVerified reputable secondary source — a well-established organisation hosting official content on the original publisher's behalf.
CUnverified third-party rehost — content usually appears genuine, but the hosting site is not the original publisher and provenance or licensing is unconfirmed. We say so explicitly in the listing description.
DLow-confidence source — meaningful accuracy, currency or provenance concerns.
EFlagged — under review, kept listed only where no better source exists.

Quality score

A 0–100 editorial score reflecting completeness (question paper and memo present, correct year/session), formatting, and how directly the link resolves to the actual document rather than a folder or index page. A high trust level does not guarantee a high quality score, and vice versa — an official source can still score lower if, for example, only the memo is available.

What "verified" means here

Each listing's last_verified date records when we last confirmed the link resolved and the metadata was accurate. It does not mean we checked the resource for correctness against the current syllabus — that responsibility stays with the teacher or learner using it.

Spotted a broken link, a wrong grade/subject tag, or a source we've rated too generously (or too harshly)? Let us know.
